Move 3D asset packs and levels from Unreal Engine to Godot 4, with meshes, materials, collision, lighting and layout. Free and open source (MIT).

It comes in two parts: an Unreal plugin that exports your level, and a Godot addon that rebuilds it. Click Export Everything in Unreal, then Import Unreal Level Layout in Godot.

This is early access (v0.5). Expect rough edges, and read "What doesn't transfer" below before you start.

What transfers

  • Level layout: positions, rotations, non-uniform scale and Blueprint actor hierarchies, converted to Godot's axes and metres. Outliner folders can be kept, and repeated Blueprints can be saved as shared .tscn prefabs.
  • Meshes: batch export of static and skeletal meshes to glTF. Animation sequences and LOD chains are optional.
  • Spline meshes: roads, pipes and fences arrive bent along their spline.
  • Collision: boxes, spheres, capsules and convex hulls become CollisionShape3D nodes, with non-uniform scale baked in so Jolt doesn't complain. Skeletal physics assets can become ragdoll bodies.
  • Materials: PBR and emissive parameters, textures, blend modes, alpha cutout and two-sided settings are bound to Godot materials. DirectX normal maps are flipped for Godot, and materials can be saved as reusable .tres files.
  • Lights and atmosphere: directional, point, spot and rect lights, plus post-process, fog and sky as a WorldEnvironment. Reflection captures become ReflectionProbe nodes.
  • Foliage: painted foliage and instanced meshes become MultiMeshInstance3D, with cull distances.
  • Landscapes: heightmaps and paint layers are rebuilt through Terrain3D, or as a simple preview mesh without it.
  • Decals, navigation volumes, and actor and component tags.
  • Cameras and ambient audio: camera actors, and ambient sounds that use a Sound Wave.

Re-importing can update an existing scene in place, keeping your own scripts and child nodes. You can also write a Godot .tscn scene directly from Unreal, cap texture sizes on import, and switch each feature on or off. Both sides write a report telling you what went wrong and where.

What doesn't transfer

  • Not converted: Blueprint graphs, gameplay logic, AnimBlueprints, particles and Sound Cue logic. This tool moves art, not behavior.
  • Materials are approximate: custom shader graph logic, such as world-position offset, vertex-paint blending or parallax, has to be rebuilt by hand.
  • Lighting won't match exactly: the engines render differently, so expect to adjust brightness.
  • Nanite: there is no Godot equivalent. Meshes arrive as regular triangle meshes.
  • Landscapes: terrain is the least mature part. Paint layers need their ground textures reassigned in Godot.
  • Loaded levels only: load World Partition cells and streaming sublevels before exporting. The report warns you about unloaded ones.

Requirements

  • Unreal Engine 5.7 or 5.8. Earlier 5.x versions are untested. Uses the built-in Python Editor Script, Editor Scripting Utilities and glTF Exporter plugins.
  • Godot 4.4 or later. Tested on 4.7.1 and 4.7.2.
  • Terrain3D (optional), recommended for landscapes.

Install

  1. Copy UnrealToGodot into your Unreal project's Plugins folder. In Edit > Plugins, enable Unreal to Godot Exporter and the three built-in plugins above, then restart.
  2. Copy addons/unreal_importer into your Godot project's addons folder. In Project Settings > Plugins, enable Unreal Engine Layout Importer.

Use it

  1. In Unreal, open your level, then Window > Unreal to Godot Exporter.
  2. Choose your output paths and click Export Everything (Meshes + Layout).
  3. In Godot, open a 3D scene and the Unreal Importer dock. Point it at the layout JSON, models and textures.
  4. Click Import Unreal Level Layout.

Large levels: keep the texture size limit on (1024 by default). Full 4K texture sets can crash Godot's importer. Also shrink texture files on disk permanently overwrites the exported copies.
